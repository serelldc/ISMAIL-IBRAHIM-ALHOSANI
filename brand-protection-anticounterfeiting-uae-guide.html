Brand Protection & Anti-Counterfeiting in the UAE

General information · Not legal advice

Counterfeiting costs brands revenue, reputation, and customer trust. The UAE gives rights holders a strong toolkit to fight back — but it only works if you use it proactively.

The UAE's primary weapon against counterfeit goods is Federal Law No. 19 of 2016 on Combating Commercial Fraud, which gives authorities broad power to seize and destroy fake products. This works alongside the Federal Trademark Law (Federal Law No. 36 of 2021), which strengthens the rights of registered trademark owners and treats deliberate, commercial-scale infringement as a criminal offense carrying fines and imprisonment.

Enforcement in the UAE is decentralized: each of the seven emirates operates its own customs authority. Five emirates — Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, and Ras Al Khaimah — maintain active customs recordal systems, with Dubai Customs' IP Rights Department (established 2005) among the most experienced in the region. A mark recorded with customs lets officers detain suspected counterfeit shipments on their own initiative, before goods ever reach the market.

A serious brand protection program combines four channels: customs recordal for border seizures, systematic monitoring of online marketplaces for fake listings, administrative raids coordinated with the Department of Economic Development, and civil or criminal proceedings against confirmed infringers. Acting on only one channel leaves obvious gaps for counterfeiters to exploit.

How Brand Protection Works, Step by Step

  1. Register your trademarkCustoms recordal, raids, and civil claims all depend on having a registered UAE trademark. Registration is the foundation of every enforcement option below.
  2. Record with UAE CustomsFile a customs recordal application in each emirate where protection is needed — Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, and Ras Al Khaimah each require a separate filing. Recordal lets customs detain suspect shipments proactively.
  3. Monitor the market and online platformsSet up ongoing monitoring of physical markets, e-commerce marketplaces, and social media for counterfeit listings using your brand name or trade dress.
  4. Send a cease-and-desist or file an administrative complaintFor identified infringers, a formal notice often resolves the matter quickly. Where it doesn't, an administrative complaint to the Department of Economic Development can trigger an inspection.
  5. Coordinate an enforcement raidEconomic Development Department inspectors, often working with police, can raid premises storing or selling counterfeit goods, seizing stock as evidence.
  6. Pursue civil or criminal actionCivil claims can secure damages, injunctions, and destruction orders. For deliberate, commercial-scale counterfeiting, a criminal complaint can result in fines and imprisonment under the Trademark Law.

When to Take Action

  • You've found counterfeit versions of your product in shops, malls, or markets.
  • Fake listings using your brand name or logo have appeared on online marketplaces.
  • A competitor is using packaging, branding, or trade dress confusingly similar to yours.
  • UAE Customs has notified you of a suspected counterfeit shipment at the border.

Frequently Asked Questions

What law governs counterfeiting in the UAE?

Federal Law No. 19 of 2016 on Combating Commercial Fraud is the primary statute, working alongside the Federal Trademark Law (Federal Law No. 36 of 2021), which criminalizes deliberate infringement.

How does UAE Customs recordal work?

Once your trademark is recorded with a customs authority, officers can detain shipments they suspect are counterfeit on their own initiative, without waiting for a complaint from you.

Do I need to record my trademark in every emirate separately?

Yes. Each emirate operates its own customs authority, and recordal in Dubai does not automatically extend protection to Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, or Ras Al Khaimah — each requires its own filing.

What penalties apply for selling counterfeit goods in the UAE?

Penalties can include seizure and destruction of goods, fines, and — for deliberate, commercial-scale counterfeiting — imprisonment under the Federal Trademark Law.

Can I take action against sellers on online marketplaces?

Yes. Most major marketplaces operating in the UAE have brand protection or takedown programs, and confirmed infringers can also face administrative complaints or civil claims.

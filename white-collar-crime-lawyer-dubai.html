White-Collar Crime Lawyer in Dubai

White-collar allegations rarely arrive as a single clean charge — they usually involve years of financial records, multiple transactions, and a prosecution theory that has to be unpicked line by line. The defense has to be built with the same level of financial detail as the case against it.

Offences such as embezzlement, breach of trust, money laundering, and commercial fraud carry both criminal exposure and, frequently, parallel civil claims for recovery — meaning a white-collar matter is rarely resolved by addressing the criminal case alone.

White-collar investigations in the UAE often begin with a complaint from an employer, business partner, or regulator, followed by document requests and financial analysis before any interview takes place. By the time the accused is formally questioned, the prosecution may already have built a substantial documentary case — which is why early engagement matters even before a formal summons arrives.

Money laundering allegations in particular carry a distinct evidentiary structure, often relying on transaction patterns and source-of-funds analysis rather than direct evidence of an underlying predicate offence. Challenging that analysis requires financial as well as legal expertise, and coordination with forensic accountants is frequently necessary.

We defend individuals and company officers accused of embezzlement, breach of trust, money laundering, bribery, and commercial fraud, working through the financial evidence in detail and coordinating with forensic and financial experts where the case requires it.

How We Handle White-Collar Crime Matters

  1. Early Case IntelligenceWe gather what is known about the complaint or investigation as early as possible, including who initiated it and what documents have already been requested.
  2. Financial Evidence ReviewWe review the underlying financial records, transactions, and accounting evidence in detail, often alongside forensic accountants where the volume or complexity warrants it.
  3. Prosecution RepresentationWe represent the accused before the Public Prosecution, addressing the specific financial allegations and challenging weak links in the documentary case.
  4. Civil Exposure CoordinationWhere a parallel civil recovery claim exists or is likely, we coordinate the criminal defense strategy with the civil exposure to avoid inconsistent positions.
  5. Trial DefenseWe build and present the defense case at trial, including expert financial evidence where it materially assists in rebutting the prosecution's theory.
  6. Appeal and Post-Conviction StrategyWhere a conviction results, we assess appeal grounds specific to the financial evidence and legal characterization applied at trial.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a civil dispute over money and a criminal fraud complaint?

A civil dispute concerns recovering money owed or damages suffered, while a criminal complaint alleges an intentional offence and can result in prosecution and penalties — the same underlying facts can sometimes give rise to both simultaneously.

Can a company director be personally prosecuted for company financial misconduct?

Yes, depending on the director's role and knowledge — personal criminal liability can attach separately from the company's own civil or regulatory exposure.

What triggers a money laundering investigation?

Common triggers include suspicious transaction reports, unusual account activity flagged by a bank, or a predicate offence investigation that expands into examining where the proceeds went.

Can a white-collar criminal matter be resolved through settlement?

In some circumstances a settlement with the complainant can influence how a matter proceeds, though this depends heavily on the nature of the offence and stage of proceedings, and is not available in all cases.

How important are financial records in defending a white-collar allegation?

Central — most white-collar cases turn on the interpretation of financial records and transaction evidence, which is why a detailed, document-led defense strategy is usually more effective than a purely narrative one.

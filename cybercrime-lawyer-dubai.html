Cybercrime Lawyer in Dubai

Cybercrime cases in the UAE are decided as much on digital evidence — device forensics, IP logs, chat records, transaction trails — as on any statement made in an interview. A defense or complaint that doesn't engage with that technical evidence directly is starting from a disadvantage.

UAE cybercrime legislation covers a wide range of conduct — from online fraud and hacking to electronic defamation and misuse of others' data — often carrying more serious penalties than equivalent offline conduct, which makes the specific legal characterization of an online act particularly consequential.

Cybercrime investigations typically involve specialized units that gather digital evidence — device seizures, forensic imaging, platform data requests — before a suspect is questioned. By the time an interview takes place, investigators frequently already have a substantial body of technical evidence, making early legal advice on how to respond critical.

Electronic defamation and privacy-related offences are increasingly common complaint types, often arising from social media posts, messaging app content, or online reviews. These cases turn heavily on context and intent, and how the original content is characterized legally can significantly affect the outcome.

We represent both individuals accused of cybercrime offences and those seeking to file complaints over online fraud, hacking, defamation, or data misuse, working closely with digital forensic considerations that are often central to these cases.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as electronic defamation under UAE law?

Content published online that damages a person's reputation can fall under electronic defamation provisions, which often carry different or more serious consequences than equivalent offline statements.

Can I be prosecuted for something posted on a private group or chat?

Potentially, yes — the fact that content was shared in a limited or private setting doesn't automatically remove it from the scope of cybercrime provisions, depending on the content and context.

What should I do if my accounts have been hacked?

Document the unauthorized access as thoroughly as possible and seek advice promptly, since a properly prepared complaint with technical detail is generally far more effective than a general report.

Can digital evidence from a personal device be used against me?

It depends on how the evidence was obtained and the specific circumstances of the case, which is why understanding the legality of any device seizure or data request is an important part of the defense.

Is online fraud treated differently from offline fraud under UAE law?

Cybercrime provisions can apply specifically to fraud conducted through electronic means, and penalties can differ from equivalent offline conduct, making the correct legal characterization important from the outset.

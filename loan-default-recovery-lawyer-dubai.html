Loan Default & Recovery Lawyer in Dubai

When a borrower stops paying, how quickly and how firmly you move often decides how much of the debt is actually recovered.

A loan default and recovery lawyer in Dubai acts for banks, financial institutions, and private lenders pursuing unpaid loans — from the first default notice through payment order proceedings, asset attachment, enforcement against collateral and guarantors, and insolvency proceedings where necessary.

UAE law gives lenders several routes to recover a defaulted loan, and the right one depends on how the debt is documented. A liquidated debt evidenced by clear supporting documents can often be pursued through the payment order (أمر الأداء) procedure, a faster summary process than ordinary litigation, while disputed or more complex debts proceed through standard civil proceedings.

Recovery is rarely just about winning a judgment — it is about turning that judgment into cash. That means identifying and attaching the borrower's bank accounts and assets, enforcing against any mortgaged property or pledged security, and pursuing guarantors where the borrower's own assets fall short.

Where a corporate borrower is genuinely insolvent, recovery may instead run through formal bankruptcy or restructuring proceedings, which change both the available remedies and the timeline. Assessing early which route actually fits the borrower's financial position saves time that a straightforward but wrong strategy would otherwise waste.

How Loan Recovery Is Handled

  1. Default Assessment & Documentation ReviewThe loan agreement, security documents, and payment history are reviewed to confirm the default, quantify the outstanding debt, and identify the fastest available recovery route.
  2. Formal Default NoticeA default notice is issued setting out the amount owed and a deadline to remedy, both to trigger contractual remedies and to support any later court application.
  3. Payment Order or Civil ClaimDepending on how the debt is documented, proceedings are filed either through the payment order procedure for liquidated debts or through a standard civil claim.
  4. Asset Tracing & AttachmentThe borrower's bank accounts, real estate, and other assets are identified and precautionary attachment orders are sought to secure them ahead of judgment.
  5. Enforcement Against Collateral & GuarantorsJudgment is enforced against mortgaged property or pledged security, and claims are pursued against any guarantors of the loan.
  6. Insolvency Proceedings (Where Applicable)Where the borrower is insolvent, recovery is pursued through formal bankruptcy or restructuring proceedings alongside or instead of ordinary enforcement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the payment order procedure and when can I use it?

It's a summary court procedure for recovering a liquidated debt evidenced by clear documentation, such as a loan agreement and statement of account — it moves faster than a standard civil claim.

Can I freeze a borrower's bank account before getting a judgment?

Yes, in appropriate cases a precautionary attachment order can be obtained to freeze bank accounts or other assets while proceedings are ongoing, to prevent the borrower dissipating funds.

What happens if the borrower doesn't have enough assets to cover the debt?

Claims can be pursued against any guarantors or co-borrowers, and where the borrower is a company, insolvency proceedings may allow recovery through a broader restructuring or liquidation process.

Is a default notice legally required before suing?

It is generally advisable and often contractually required — it creates a clear record of default and gives the borrower a final opportunity to pay before formal proceedings begin.

How long does loan recovery typically take?

It depends on the route: payment order proceedings for undisputed liquidated debts are relatively fast, while disputed claims, asset enforcement, or insolvency proceedings take longer.

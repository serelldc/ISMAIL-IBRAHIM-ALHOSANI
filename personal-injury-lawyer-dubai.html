Personal Injury Lawyer in Dubai

An accident that leaves you injured creates both a medical recovery to manage and a legal claim to prove — and the two run on very different timelines.

A personal injury lawyer in Dubai pursues compensation on behalf of individuals injured in road traffic accidents, workplace accidents, and other incidents caused by another party's fault, working alongside the medical and insurance processes that follow.

Personal injury claims in the UAE often begin with a police report, particularly for road traffic and workplace accidents, and that report frequently becomes central evidence in both any criminal or administrative process and the separate civil claim for compensation. Medical documentation — treatment records, specialist assessments, and where relevant, a permanent disability rating — is equally central, since compensation is tied directly to the nature and extent of the injury.

Where a vehicle or employer's insurance is involved, the claim frequently proceeds partly through the insurer and partly through the courts, and understanding how those two tracks interact is often decisive in how quickly and fully a claim is resolved. In cases of death or serious permanent injury, UAE law also recognises diya and related compensation concepts alongside ordinary civil damages, adding a further layer to how the claim is structured.

Because medical treatment is often ongoing at the time a claim is being assessed, timing matters: pursuing a claim too early can undervalue an injury whose full extent is not yet known, while waiting too long risks running into limitation periods. Getting this balance right, together with securing the right medical and, where needed, accident-reconstruction evidence, shapes the outcome as much as the underlying facts of the accident itself.

How Personal Injury Claims Are Handled

  1. Accident & Police Report ReviewThe circumstances of the accident and any police or incident report are reviewed to establish fault and the sequence of events.
  2. Medical Evidence GatheringTreatment records, specialist assessments, and disability ratings are gathered to document the nature and extent of the injury.
  3. Insurance CoordinationWhere vehicle or employer insurance is involved, the claim is coordinated with the insurer alongside any separate civil proceedings.
  4. Claim ValuationThe claim is valued based on medical evidence, lost income, and, in appropriate cases, diya or related compensation concepts.
  5. Filing & Court ProceedingsWhere the matter is not resolved through insurance or negotiation, the claim is filed and proceeds through the civil courts.
  6. Judgment & RecoveryFollowing judgment or settlement, compensation is pursued through payment or, where necessary, execution proceedings.

When You Need a Personal Injury Lawyer

  • You were injured in a road traffic accident caused by another driver.

  • You are unsure whether to accept an insurer's settlement offer for your injury.
  • You have a permanent disability resulting from an accident and need it properly valued.
  • A family member has died or been seriously injured in an accident and you need advice on diya and compensation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a police report to bring a personal injury claim?

For road traffic and many workplace accidents, a police or incident report is important evidence and is usually the starting point for both any related process and the civil claim.

Should I accept the insurer's first settlement offer?

Not necessarily — an early offer may not reflect the full extent of the injury, especially where treatment or a disability assessment is still ongoing.

What is diya and when does it apply?

Diya is a compensation concept recognised under UAE law in cases of death or serious injury, applied alongside ordinary civil damages in appropriate cases.

How is a permanent disability valued in a claim?

A formal disability assessment or rating is generally required, and this rating plays a significant role in how the overall claim is valued.

Is there a time limit to bring a personal injury claim?

Yes — limitation periods apply, so it is important not to delay too long while treatment or negotiations are ongoing.

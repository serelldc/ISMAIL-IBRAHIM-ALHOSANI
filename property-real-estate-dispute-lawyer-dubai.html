Property & Real Estate Dispute Lawyer in Dubai

Property disputes in Dubai often run through their own specialised bodies before they ever reach the ordinary civil courts — knowing which route applies can be as important as the merits of the dispute itself.

A property and real estate dispute lawyer in Dubai handles disputes over off-plan developments, title and ownership, tenancy, and jointly owned property, working across the Dubai Land Department, RERA, the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre, and the civil courts as the case requires.

Real estate disputes in Dubai are not a single track. Tenancy disputes generally go through the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre rather than the ordinary courts, off-plan project issues often engage the Real Estate Regulatory Agency and escrow account rules, and disputes over jointly owned property, such as service charges or owners' association decisions, can involve a separate regulatory process again. Identifying the correct forum at the outset avoids delay and wasted procedural steps.

Off-plan disputes typically centre on delayed handover, project cancellation, and the developer's obligations under the sale and purchase agreement and escrow arrangements, while ownership disputes often turn on Dubai Land Department registration records and the documentary chain of title. Tenancy disputes commonly involve eviction, rent increases, maintenance obligations, and security deposit disagreements, each governed by its own specific rules and notice requirements.

Because ownership, tenancy, and off-plan structures are often documented through standard-form contracts and government registration systems, much of the dispute turns on how those records and contractual terms are read and applied — making an accurate reading of the registration and contractual position, alongside the correct procedural route, central to resolving these disputes efficiently.

How Property Disputes Are Handled

  1. Dispute Classification & Forum IdentificationThe dispute is assessed to determine whether it belongs before the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre, RERA, the Dubai Land Department, or the civil courts.
  2. Documentation & Registration ReviewTitle deeds, sale and purchase agreements, tenancy contracts, and Dubai Land Department records are reviewed to establish the relevant rights and obligations.
  3. Notice & Pre-Action StepsAny required notices — such as eviction notices or default notices under the sale contract — are reviewed or issued to satisfy procedural requirements.
  4. Filing Before the Correct BodyThe claim is filed before the appropriate forum, whether the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre, RERA, or the civil courts.
  5. Hearings & SubmissionsThe matter proceeds through hearings and submissions before the relevant body, addressing the specific property or tenancy issue in dispute.
  6. Decision & EnforcementFollowing the decision or judgment, compliance or payment is pursued, including registration updates or execution proceedings where necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all property disputes go to the same court?

No — tenancy disputes generally go through the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre, off-plan and regulatory issues often involve RERA, and other disputes may proceed through the ordinary civil courts, depending on the nature of the claim.

What can I do if my off-plan property handover is delayed?

Options depend on the terms of the sale and purchase agreement and applicable RERA rules, and may include compensation, project-related remedies, or in some cases cancellation.

How are ownership disputes resolved?

These generally turn on the Dubai Land Department's registration records and the documentary chain of title, alongside any relevant contractual agreements.

Can I dispute service charges set by an owners' association?

Yes, in appropriate circumstances service charges and related decisions can be challenged through the relevant regulatory process.

What notice is required before an eviction?

Eviction generally requires proper notice under the applicable tenancy rules, and the specific requirements depend on the grounds for eviction.

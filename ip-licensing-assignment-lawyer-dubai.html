IP Licensing & Assignment Lawyer in Dubai

Monetizing intellectual property often means either licensing it for others to use under defined terms or assigning ownership outright — two transactions with very different legal and commercial consequences.

A license grants permission to use IP under specific terms while ownership stays with the original holder, whereas an assignment transfers ownership itself, and the distinction has significant implications for control and revenue.

Licensing terms need to define scope precisely — which rights are granted, in what territory, for what duration, and whether the license is exclusive or can be granted to multiple parties simultaneously.

Royalty and payment structures in a licensing agreement should reflect how the licensed IP will actually generate value, whether through a flat fee, ongoing royalties, or a combination structure.

We structure licensing agreements that protect the rights holder's interests while providing workable terms for the licensee, and handle assignment transactions where full ownership transfer is the goal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between licensing and assigning intellectual property?

Licensing grants permission to use the IP under defined terms while ownership stays with the original holder, whereas assignment transfers ownership itself to the other party.

What should a licensing agreement's scope actually define?

It should precisely define which rights are granted, in what territory, for what duration, and whether the license is exclusive or non-exclusive, since ambiguity here often leads to later disputes.

How should royalty terms be structured in a licensing agreement?

This depends on how the licensed IP will generate value — options include a flat fee, ongoing royalties tied to revenue or usage, or a combination structure, and we help negotiate terms that work for both parties.

Do licenses and assignments need to be registered?

Depending on the type of IP and jurisdiction, registration or recordal can be important to give the arrangement effect against third parties, and we advise on when this is necessary.

What can be done if a licensee isn't complying with the agreement's terms?

Options depend on the specific breach and the agreement's terms, ranging from formal notice to termination of the license or further legal action.

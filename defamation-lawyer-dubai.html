Defamation Lawyer in Dubai

Defamation in the UAE carries criminal exposure, not just civil liability — a complaint over a damaging statement can lead to prosecution, which changes the stakes considerably compared to jurisdictions where defamation is purely a civil matter. Both sides need to understand this from the outset.

Defamation claims turn heavily on context — what was said, to whom, in what setting, and whether it can be substantiated as true. A statement that seems minor out of context can carry real legal exposure, while a serious-sounding accusation may have a complete defense if it can be proven accurate.

Defamation in the UAE can arise from spoken statements, written communications, or online content, and the applicable legal framework treats these somewhat differently depending on the medium and context. Truth can be a defense in some circumstances, but is not an absolute shield, particularly where the manner of the statement itself causes harm independent of its accuracy.

Business and workplace disputes frequently give rise to defamation complaints — a negative review, an internal complaint shared beyond its intended audience, or a public accusation of misconduct. These cases require careful attention to both what was actually said and the surrounding circumstances that may support or undermine a defense.

We represent both complainants pursuing defamation claims and individuals defending against them, assessing the strength of the underlying statement, the available defenses, and the most effective procedural path given the case's specific facts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is defamation a criminal or civil matter in the UAE?

It can carry criminal exposure in addition to any civil claim for damages, which is a significant difference from jurisdictions where defamation is handled purely through civil courts.

Is truth always a complete defense to a defamation complaint?

Not necessarily — while truth is relevant, the manner and circumstances in which a true statement was made can still be relevant to a claim in certain situations, so it isn't automatically a complete defense.

Can I be accused of defamation for a negative online review?

Potentially, yes, particularly if the review contains false factual claims rather than genuine opinion — the distinction between fact and opinion is often central to these cases.

What evidence do I need to support a defamation complaint?

You generally need to establish what was said or published, that it was communicated to others, and that it caused or was likely to cause reputational harm — screenshots, witness statements, and records of the statement are typically central.

Can a defamation matter be resolved without going to trial?

In some cases, an apology, retraction, or settlement can resolve the matter before trial, though this depends on the stage of proceedings and both parties' willingness to reach an agreement.

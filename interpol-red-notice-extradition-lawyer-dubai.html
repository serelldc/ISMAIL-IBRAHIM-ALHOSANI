Interpol Red Notice & Extradition Lawyer in Dubai

An Interpol Red Notice or extradition request can disrupt travel, banking, and reputation long before any court has ruled on the underlying allegation — early legal intervention changes the outcome.

An Interpol Red Notice and extradition lawyer in Dubai advises individuals who are the subject of, or at risk of, an Interpol Red Notice, Diffusion, or formal extradition request — from an urgent initial risk assessment through to a request for deletion before Interpol's Commission for the Control of Files (CCF), or a defense against extradition before the UAE courts.

A Red Notice is a request issued by Interpol's General Secretariat, at the request of a member country, asking law enforcement worldwide to locate and provisionally arrest a person pending extradition. It is not an international arrest warrant, and member countries decide individually whether to act on it — but in practice a Red Notice can trigger detention at a border or airport, frozen bank accounts, and refused visas, often without the subject having any advance warning.

The firm advises clients at every stage: assessing whether a Red Notice or Diffusion exists in their name before they travel, preparing and filing requests for deletion or correction with Interpol's CCF where a notice was issued in breach of Interpol's own rules, and representing clients who have been provisionally arrested in the UAE pending a formal extradition request, including before the UAE courts that ultimately decide whether extradition will proceed.

Dubai's role as a major international travel and business hub means exposure to an Interpol alert can surface without warning, at an airport or during a routine bank review. Because both the CCF process and extradition proceedings run on firm procedural timelines, acting early — ideally before travel, not after an arrest — materially improves the available options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is an Interpol Red Notice?

A Red Notice is a request issued by Interpol at a member country's request, asking law enforcement worldwide to locate and provisionally arrest a person pending extradition. It is not itself an international arrest warrant — each member country decides independently whether to act on it.

Can I be arrested in Dubai because of a Red Notice?

Yes. The UAE is an Interpol member country, and a Red Notice can lead to provisional arrest at a UAE border or airport pending a decision on whether to proceed with extradition.

How do I get a Red Notice removed?

By filing a formal request for deletion with Interpol's Commission for the Control of Files (CCF), typically on the basis that the notice breaches Interpol's own rules — for example where it is predominantly political, military, religious, or racial in character, which Interpol's Constitution prohibits, or where the underlying charge does not meet Interpol's requirements.

How long does the CCF process take?

Timelines vary, but a standard CCF request for deletion generally takes several months to resolve; an urgent procedure is available in limited circumstances involving imminent, serious harm.

Will I automatically be extradited if a Red Notice exists against me?

No. A Red Notice alone does not result in automatic extradition. Extradition is a separate, formal legal process requiring review by the UAE courts of the requesting state's application, the applicable treaty basis, and any available defenses.

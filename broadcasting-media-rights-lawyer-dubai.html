Broadcasting & Media Rights Lawyer in Dubai

Broadcasting rights are often the single most valuable asset a sports property holds — a media rights lawyer protects that value from the licence agreement through to enforcement against unauthorised streams.

A broadcasting and media rights lawyer in Dubai structures and negotiates the licensing of live sports broadcast, streaming, and content rights, and enforces those rights against unauthorised distribution and piracy.

Broadcasting and media rights in the UAE sit at the intersection of copyright protection under Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2021 on Copyrights and Neighbouring Rights and general commercial contract law, with content distribution and broadcasting activity additionally subject to oversight by the UAE Media Council.

The firm acts for rights holders — federations, clubs, and event organisers — licensing broadcast and streaming rights to their competitions, and for broadcasters and streaming platforms acquiring those rights, negotiating scope, exclusivity, territory, and delivery terms on both sides of the transaction.

Dubai's calendar of major international sporting fixtures generates broadcast rights deals of significant commercial value, and that value can erode quickly once content is pirated or re-streamed without authorisation, making early rights structuring and active enforcement equally important.

How a Broadcasting & Media Rights Matter Works

  1. Rights Audit & Ownership VerificationThe lawyer confirms the chain of title to the underlying broadcast rights before any licence is granted, avoiding disputes over who is entitled to sell what.
  2. Deal StructuringTerritory, exclusivity, platform (linear TV, streaming, digital clips), term, and any permitted sub-licensing are defined to match the commercial objective.
  3. Licence Agreement Drafting & NegotiationThe licence agreement is drafted and negotiated, covering fees, minimum guarantees, delivery specifications, and remedies for default.
  4. Regulatory & Compliance ReviewContent and distribution plans are checked against UAE Media Council requirements before broadcast to avoid compliance issues.
  5. Monitoring for Unauthorised UseOnce content airs, the rights holder monitors for piracy or unauthorised re-streaming across platforms.
  6. Enforcement ActionWhere infringement is found, the lawyer pursues platform takedown notices and, where necessary, court or arbitration action against the infringing party.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who owns broadcast rights to a sporting event in the UAE?

Ownership generally rests with the event organiser or governing federation under the applicable competition rules, distinct from any separate copyright a broadcaster may hold in footage it films and produces.

Can I stop unauthorised streams of my event?

Yes — through platform takedown notices under applicable copyright law, direct enforcement correspondence, and, in serious or repeated cases, court or arbitration proceedings against the infringing party.

What law governs broadcast content in the UAE?

Content distribution and broadcasting activity are regulated by the UAE Media Council, while the underlying rights are protected under Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2021 on Copyrights and Neighbouring Rights.

Can I license my event's rights to multiple broadcasters in different territories?

Yes, provided the licence agreements clearly define territorial scope and exclusivity so the grants do not overlap or conflict with one another.

What happens if a broadcaster breaches the licence agreement?

Remedies typically include termination rights, damages, and in some cases injunctive relief, depending on the terms negotiated in the licence agreement itself.

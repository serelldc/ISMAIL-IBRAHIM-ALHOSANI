Child Custody Lawyer in Dubai

Custody decisions in the UAE follow personal status law principles that differ in important respects from the systems many expatriate parents are familiar with — understanding the framework early shapes how a custody claim should be built.

UAE law generally distinguishes between custody, meaning day-to-day care of the child, and guardianship, meaning legal decision-making authority over matters like education and travel — the two are not automatically held by the same parent, and understanding this distinction is central to any custody case.

Custody determinations consider the age and needs of the child, the fitness and circumstances of each parent, and the practical arrangements each parent can offer, alongside the legal principles that apply under UAE personal status law.

For expatriate parents, custody cases often intersect with questions about relocation, travel permissions, and how a custody order will be recognized if a parent later moves abroad — these issues benefit from being addressed at the outset rather than after a dispute arises.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between custody and guardianship under UAE law?

Custody generally refers to day-to-day physical care of the child, while guardianship refers to legal decision-making authority over matters such as education, travel, and finances — the two roles can be held by different parents.

Can a custody order be changed after it's issued?

Yes, custody arrangements can generally be modified where there's a material change in circumstances affecting the child's welfare or a parent's ability to care for them.

Do I need my ex-spouse's permission to travel abroad with our child?

Depending on the custody and guardianship arrangement, travel with a child may require the other parent's consent or a court-issued travel permission, so this should be addressed clearly in the custody order.

How does the court decide who gets custody?

The court considers the child's best interests, including age and needs, alongside applicable legal principles and each parent's circumstances and ability to provide care.

What happens to custody arrangements if a parent relocates abroad?

Relocation can significantly affect an existing custody arrangement, and it's important to address this possibility in the original order or seek modification before relocating where required.

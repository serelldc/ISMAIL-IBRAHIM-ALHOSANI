Inheritance & Probate Lawyer in Dubai

Inheritance in the UAE defaults to Sharia-based succession rules unless steps are taken in advance to apply a different framework, which makes early planning — and, when a death has already occurred, prompt legal advice — significant for how an estate is actually distributed.

Non-Muslim foreign nationals in the UAE can in many cases elect to have their home country's inheritance law apply to their UAE assets, but this generally requires proactive registration or documentation — without it, default succession rules may apply regardless of the deceased's wishes or nationality.

Probate for UAE-based assets, including real estate and bank accounts, generally requires a formal court process to establish heirs and authorize distribution, and this process differs depending on whether the deceased had a registered will or applicable foreign law election in place.

For expatriates with assets in multiple countries, coordinating UAE probate with proceedings in other jurisdictions adds complexity, particularly where a will was drafted abroad and needs to be recognized or given effect within the UAE system.

We advise clients on structuring their estate in advance to achieve their intended distribution, and represent heirs and estate representatives through the probate process following a death, including cross-border coordination where relevant.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to my UAE assets if I die without a will?

Without a valid will or applicable law election, default succession rules generally apply, which may not distribute assets according to your personal wishes, making advance planning valuable.

Can a foreign national choose which country's inheritance law applies to their UAE estate?

In many cases, non-Muslim foreign nationals can elect to have their home country's law apply, though this generally requires proactive registration or documentation to take effect.

How long does the probate process typically take in the UAE?

Timelines vary depending on the complexity of the estate, whether a valid will exists, and whether the case involves cross-border elements, so it's difficult to generalize without reviewing the specific circumstances.

Do I need a separate will for my UAE assets if I already have one in my home country?

It's often advisable to have a UAE-registered will addressing UAE-based assets specifically, as this can simplify the local probate process considerably compared to relying solely on a foreign will.

Can heirs contest a will in the UAE?

Yes, disputes over a will's validity or an estate's distribution can be raised through the appropriate legal process, and we represent clients on both sides of such disputes.

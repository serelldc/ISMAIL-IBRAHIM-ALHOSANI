Guardianship & Parental Rights Lawyer in Dubai

Guardianship governs who has the legal authority to make major decisions for a child — school enrollment, medical treatment, travel, financial matters — and this authority doesn't automatically sit with whichever parent has day-to-day custody.

Under UAE personal status law, guardianship and custody are treated as distinct legal roles that can be held by different parents, meaning the custodial parent caring for a child day to day may still need the guardian's consent for significant decisions like enrolling in school or obtaining a passport.

Disputes over guardianship frequently arise around specific decisions — a school choice, a medical procedure, a travel plan — where the custodial and guardian parents disagree, and resolving these disputes requires understanding exactly which decisions fall within guardianship authority.

For expatriate families in particular, guardianship questions often intersect with practical necessities like passport renewals, school enrollment paperwork, and travel consent, where delays caused by unresolved guardianship disputes can have real consequences.

We advise and represent parents on guardianship rights and obligations, resolving disputes over specific decisions and, where necessary, pursuing court intervention to clarify or modify guardianship arrangements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between custody and guardianship?

Custody generally refers to day-to-day physical care of the child, while guardianship refers to legal decision-making authority over matters like education, medical care, and travel — the two roles can be held by different parents.

Do I need the guardian's consent to enroll my child in school?

This depends on the specific custody and guardianship arrangement, but school enrollment often requires the guardian's consent or involvement, so this should be clarified early.

Can guardianship be transferred from one parent to another?

Yes, guardianship arrangements can generally be modified where there's a material change in circumstances or where it serves the child's welfare.

What happens if the guardian and custodial parent can't agree on a medical decision?

Where negotiation doesn't resolve the disagreement, court intervention can be sought to rule on the specific disputed decision, particularly where time-sensitive medical needs are involved.

Does a child need both parents' consent to travel internationally?

This depends on the specific custody and guardianship arrangement in place, and travel consent requirements should be clearly addressed in any custody or guardianship order to avoid disputes.

What to Do If Your IP Is Infringed in the UAE

General information · Not legal advice

Finding a counterfeit product or a copied design doesn't leave you without options — the UAE offers several enforcement routes, from a cease-and-desist letter to customs seizure and criminal prosecution.

IP owners in the UAE have several enforcement channels available, and the right combination depends on how the infringement is happening — a shipment of counterfeit goods, an unauthorized reseller, or copied content online each call for a different first move. Acting quickly matters, since evidence can disappear and infringing stock can be sold before a formal process concludes.

For trademarks specifically, Federal Decree-Law No. 36 of 2021 on Trademarks makes willful infringement a criminal offense carrying fines and imprisonment, in addition to the civil remedies of injunctions and damages available for trademark, copyright, and patent infringement generally.

Border enforcement is often the most effective tool against counterfeit imports. Recording a trademark or copyrighted work with UAE Customs enables an ex officio inspection system, meaning customs can flag and detain suspicious shipments on its own initiative rather than waiting for a complaint about a specific consignment.

Enforcement Options When Your IP Is Infringed

  1. Cease-and-Desist NoticeA formal notice to the infringing party is often the fastest way to stop unauthorized use, and creates a documented record if further action becomes necessary.
  2. Customs RecordalRecording a trademark or copyrighted work with UAE Customs enables proactive detention of suspected counterfeit shipments at the border.
  3. Administrative ComplaintA complaint to the Ministry of Economy or the relevant Economic Development Department can trigger an inspection or raid against premises selling infringing goods.
  4. Civil ClaimA civil claim before the courts can secure an injunction to stop the infringement and damages for the harm caused.
  5. Criminal ComplaintFor willful infringement, a criminal complaint can lead to fines and imprisonment under the applicable IP laws, in addition to any civil remedy pursued.
  6. Online TakedownPlatforms hosting infringing listings, counterfeit product pages, or fake accounts can be approached directly for removal, often alongside the other enforcement steps.

Signs You Should Act on IP Infringement

  • You've found counterfeit or near-identical products being sold under your brand.
  • A shipment you suspect is counterfeit is due to pass through a UAE port or airport.
  • Your copyrighted content, product images, or designs are being used without permission online.
  • A former distributor, licensee, or employee continues using your IP after the relationship has ended.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is customs recordal and why does it matter?

Recording a trademark or copyrighted work with UAE Customs allows border authorities to proactively detain suspected counterfeit shipments, rather than requiring the rights holder to identify each shipment individually.

What happens after customs detains a suspicious shipment?

The rights holder is notified and typically has a short window, often just a few working days, to file a formal complaint confirming the goods are infringing before customs can act further.

Can trademark infringement lead to criminal penalties in the UAE?

Yes. Willful trademark infringement is a criminal offense under Federal Decree-Law No. 36 of 2021, which can carry significant fines and imprisonment, separate from any civil claim for damages.

What can I do about infringing content online?

Many platforms have takedown procedures for counterfeit listings, unauthorized use of copyrighted images, or fake brand accounts, and a formal notice from the rights holder often speeds up removal.

Do I need to have registered my trademark or copyright before enforcing it?

For trademarks, registration strengthens enforcement significantly and is required for some remedies. Copyright arises automatically, but a registration certificate makes proving ownership in a dispute considerably easier.

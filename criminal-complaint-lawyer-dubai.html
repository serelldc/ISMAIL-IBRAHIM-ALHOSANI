Criminal Complaint Lawyer in Dubai

Filing a criminal complaint in the UAE sets a formal process in motion that's difficult to withdraw once underway, and the way it's framed at the outset shapes how the police and prosecution treat it. The same is true in reverse — the person facing a poorly framed complaint needs to challenge it early, not just at trial.

Many disputes that begin as civil or commercial disagreements — a bounced cheque, a failed business partnership, a family dispute — can also give rise to a criminal complaint under UAE law, and understanding whether a matter has both civil and criminal dimensions changes the entire strategy.

A criminal complaint in the UAE is typically lodged with the police, who conduct an initial inquiry before referring the matter to the Public Prosecution for a formal investigation. How the complaint is framed at the police stage — the specific facts alleged and the legal characterization sought — significantly influences the direction the case takes from that point forward.

For someone facing a complaint, an early and well-prepared response to the police or prosecution can sometimes prevent a matter from escalating to formal charges, particularly where the complaint rests on a misunderstanding, a civil dispute framed as criminal conduct, or insufficient evidence of intent.

We assist both complainants seeking to file a criminal complaint and individuals responding to one, preparing the initial complaint or response with the specific legal framing the matter requires, and representing clients through the police and prosecutorial stages that follow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a civil dispute also be the basis of a criminal complaint?

Yes — certain conduct, such as issuing a bounced cheque or breach of trust involving deception, can give rise to both civil liability and criminal exposure under UAE law simultaneously.

Can a criminal complaint be withdrawn once filed?

In some cases yes, though the procedural effect of withdrawal depends on the stage the case has reached and the specific offence involved — it isn't always a full stop to proceedings.

What happens if I ignore a criminal complaint filed against me?

The police or prosecution can proceed with the investigation regardless, and failing to respond or attend when required can create additional complications, including possible detention orders.

Do I need evidence to file a criminal complaint?

You need enough factual basis for the police to open an inquiry, though the strength of that basis significantly affects how quickly and seriously the complaint is pursued.

Can I file both a civil claim and a criminal complaint over the same dispute?

In many cases yes, though how the two interact procedurally and which to pursue first depends heavily on the specific facts and the objective you're trying to achieve.

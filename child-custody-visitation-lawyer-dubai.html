Child Custody & Visitation Lawyer in Dubai

A custody order alone rarely resolves every practical question — visitation schedules, where handovers happen, how holidays are split — and disputes over these details often cause more ongoing friction than the original custody decision.

Visitation arrangements in the UAE can be set out with as much or as little detail as the parties agree, and vague or informal arrangements are frequently the source of repeated disputes — a clearly drafted schedule addressing routine time, holidays, and travel tends to prevent far more conflict than it creates.

Where parents can't agree on a workable schedule, the court will set visitation terms, and the specificity of that order matters considerably — ambiguous terms about timing or location often lead to further disputes down the line.

Visitation issues frequently arise alongside relocation questions, changes in a parent's work schedule, or a child's changing needs as they grow older, all of which may justify revisiting an existing arrangement.

We assist parents in establishing clear, workable visitation arrangements, contesting unreasonable restrictions on access, and enforcing visitation rights that aren't being honored by the other parent.

How We Handle Visitation Matters

  1. Family Situation and Needs AssessmentWe review the family's circumstances, the child's schedule and needs, and any existing custody arrangement to identify what a workable visitation schedule requires.
  2. Schedule Drafting or NegotiationWe draft or negotiate detailed visitation terms covering routine time, holidays, and special occasions, aiming to minimize future ambiguity.
  3. Court Application Where NeededWhere agreement isn't possible, we apply to the court for a visitation order, presenting the practical case for the schedule sought.
  4. Handling Access RestrictionsWhere a custodial parent is unreasonably restricting access, we pursue the appropriate legal steps to secure the visitation rights owed.
  5. Modification for Changed CircumstancesWe assist with modifying visitation arrangements as work schedules, relocations, or the child's needs change over time.
  6. Enforcement of Visitation RightsWhere visitation orders aren't being honored, we pursue enforcement to secure compliance.

When to Bring in a Visitation Lawyer

  • You need a clear, detailed visitation schedule established as part of or following a custody arrangement.
  • The other parent is restricting or denying your access to your child without justification.
  • Your work schedule or living situation has changed and your current visitation arrangement no longer works.
  • You want to formalize an informal visitation arrangement to avoid future disputes.
  • Your child's needs or schedule have changed as they've gotten older, requiring an updated arrangement.
  • You need to enforce a visitation order the other parent isn't complying with.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if the other parent won't let me see my child?

Unreasonable denial of visitation can be addressed through the appropriate legal process, and we help clients pursue enforcement of their existing rights or establish clear terms where none currently exist.

Can a visitation schedule be changed later?

Yes, visitation arrangements can generally be modified where circumstances such as work schedules, relocation, or the child's needs have materially changed.

Should visitation arrangements be put in writing even if we're on good terms?

Yes, formalizing arrangements in writing, even amicable ones, helps prevent misunderstandings and provides clarity if circumstances or relationships change later.

How are holidays typically handled in a visitation schedule?

Holiday schedules are usually addressed specifically and separately from the routine schedule, often alternating between parents year to year to ensure fairness.

What can I do if the other parent consistently doesn't follow the agreed visitation schedule?

Repeated non-compliance can be pursued through enforcement proceedings, and documenting the pattern of non-compliance is an important part of building that case.

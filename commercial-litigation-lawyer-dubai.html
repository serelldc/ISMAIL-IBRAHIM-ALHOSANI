Commercial Litigation Lawyer in Dubai

When a commercial relationship breaks down and negotiation fails, the dispute moves into the UAE court system — where procedure, evidence, and timing determine outcomes as much as the underlying merits of the claim.

A commercial litigation lawyer in Dubai represents businesses in contentious commercial disputes before the UAE courts — breach of contract, non-payment, agency and distribution termination, and unfair competition claims — from the first filing through appeal and enforcement.

Commercial litigation covers the full range of disputes that can arise between businesses once a relationship has broken down: a customer refusing to pay an invoice, a supplier failing to deliver as agreed, a distributor terminated without the compensation the law or contract requires, or a competitor engaging in conduct that unfairly damages another business. Unlike shareholder disputes, which concern conflicts between the owners of a company, commercial litigation concerns disputes between separate businesses dealing with each other at arm's length.

The firm represents claimants and defendants before the Dubai courts at every stage — filing or defending a claim before the Court of First Instance, appealing to the Court of Appeal, and, where necessary, pursuing further appeal to the Court of Cassation on points of law. This includes securing interim measures where assets are at risk, managing expert appointments that UAE commercial litigation frequently relies on, and enforcing judgments once obtained.

Commercial litigation in the UAE moves on court-set timelines and is often shaped heavily by court-appointed experts, whose findings can determine the outcome as much as legal argument. Early case preparation — securing evidence, understanding the applicable procedure, and setting realistic expectations — has a significant bearing on how a case unfolds.

How We Handle Commercial Litigation

  1. Claim AssessmentWe review the underlying facts, contracts, and correspondence to assess the strength of a potential claim or defence and the realistic range of outcomes.
  2. Pre-Action CorrespondenceWhere appropriate, we send formal notice setting out the claim and inviting settlement, which can resolve a dispute without the cost and delay of litigation.
  3. Filing or Defending the ClaimWe prepare and file the statement of claim, or the defence and any counterclaim, before the competent Court of First Instance.
  4. Evidence & Expert ProceedingsWe manage the evidentiary process, including the court-appointed expert examinations that are central to most UAE commercial litigation, submitting technical memoranda to support the client's position.
  5. Hearings & JudgmentWe represent the client at hearings and submissions before the court, through to first-instance judgment, and advise on the merits of appeal if the outcome is unfavourable.
  6. Appeal & EnforcementWhere necessary, we pursue or defend an appeal before the Court of Appeal, and take the steps needed to enforce a favourable judgment against the losing party's assets.

When You Need a Commercial Litigation Lawyer

  • A customer or business partner is refusing to pay an invoice or perform its obligations under a contract.
  • You have been served with a claim before the UAE courts and need to file a defence.
  • A distributor or agency relationship has been terminated and you dispute the compensation offered — or are the party facing such a claim.
  • A competitor or business contact is engaging in conduct that is unfairly damaging your business.
  • You have obtained a favourable judgment and need to enforce it against a debtor's assets.
  • You have lost at first instance and are considering whether to appeal.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does commercial litigation take in Dubai?

It varies significantly with complexity, but a first-instance commercial case commonly takes between six months and two years, with expert proceedings often the largest factor in how long a case takes.

What is the role of a court-appointed expert in a commercial case?

UAE courts frequently appoint an independent expert to examine technical or financial questions — such as the amount owed under a contract — and the expert's report often carries substantial weight in the court's final judgment.

Can I get an injunction to freeze a defendant's assets before judgment?

In appropriate cases, yes. UAE law allows for precautionary attachment over a defendant's assets or bank accounts where there is a genuine risk that a future judgment could not otherwise be enforced.

What is the difference between this and a shareholder dispute?

Commercial litigation concerns disputes between separate, unrelated businesses — such as a customer and supplier. A shareholder dispute concerns a conflict between the owners of the same company, which typically involves different legal mechanisms and remedies.

Can a UAE court judgment be enforced against assets abroad?

It depends on the country. Enforcement abroad depends on bilateral treaties or reciprocal enforcement arrangements between the UAE and the relevant country, and the process differs from enforcing an arbitral award under the New York Convention.

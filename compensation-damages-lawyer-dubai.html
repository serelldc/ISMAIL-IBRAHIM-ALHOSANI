Compensation & Damages Lawyer in Dubai

Winning on liability is only half the case — compensation must still be proven, item by item, before a court will actually award it.

A compensation and damages lawyer in Dubai builds and defends claims for financial and moral compensation arising from civil wrongs, focusing on how loss is proven, quantified, and awarded under UAE law.

UAE civil law follows a compensatory principle: an award is meant to make good the actual loss suffered, not to punish the wrongdoer or hand the claimant a windfall. This means every element of a compensation claim — the wrongful act, the loss, and the causal link between them — has to be established and, where possible, quantified with supporting documentation.

Compensation can cover both material loss, such as direct financial damage and lost profit, and moral damage, recognising harm to reputation, feelings, or standing, though moral damages are assessed more conservatively and require their own justification. Claimants are also expected to take reasonable steps to mitigate their loss, and a failure to do so can reduce the amount ultimately recovered.

Because UAE courts frequently rely on court-appointed experts to assess and quantify loss, the quality of the evidence submitted — invoices, valuations, financial records, correspondence — often determines the final award more than the legal argument itself. Poorly evidenced claims are routinely reduced by the expert regardless of how strong the underlying liability case is.

How Compensation Claims Are Handled

  1. Loss Identification & CategorisationThe claimant's losses are identified and separated into material and moral heads of damage, each requiring different types of evidence.
  2. Causation AnalysisThe causal link between the wrongful act and each head of loss is analysed, since compensation is only available for loss actually caused by the wrong.
  3. Evidence & QuantificationSupporting evidence — invoices, valuations, financial statements, and expert reports — is gathered to quantify the claim as precisely as possible.
  4. Mitigation ReviewThe steps taken (or that should have been taken) to limit the loss are reviewed, since a failure to mitigate can reduce the amount recoverable.
  5. Filing & Expert ExaminationThe claim is filed and, where a court-appointed expert is assigned, evidence is presented and defended during the expert examination process.
  6. Award & EnforcementFollowing judgment, the compensation awarded is reviewed against what was claimed, with enforcement pursued where necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does UAE law allow punitive damages?

No — compensation is generally limited to the actual proven loss suffered, and is not intended to punish the wrongdoer beyond that.

What is the difference between material and moral damages?

Material damage covers direct financial loss and lost profit, while moral damage covers non-financial harm such as reputational or emotional harm, and is assessed more conservatively.

Do I need an expert to prove my damages?

In most cases the court appoints its own expert to assess and quantify loss, and the evidence submitted to that expert plays a central role in the final award.

What happens if I didn't try to reduce my losses?

A failure to take reasonable steps to mitigate loss can result in a reduction of the compensation ultimately awarded.

I've already won on liability — why do I still need to prove my damages separately?

Establishing that a wrong occurred and proving the value of the resulting loss are separate exercises, and courts require specific evidence for the latter before awarding compensation.
